Setup

The original paper characterised coordinated hallucination (C-Hall) — two cheap models agreeing on the same wrong answer — as a split between knowledge (≈9%) and math (31.8%). The 20 August report then asked whether model-family relatedness drives C-Hall, and found the pairwise differences indistinguishable from zero at N = 500/pair.

This work adds four benchmarks — TruthfulQA (adversarial misconceptions), MMLU-Pro (harder, up to 10 options), OpenBookQA and CommonsenseQA — and four capability-matched pairs, each holding its frontier constant so family relatedness is the only manipulated variable:

01Generalisation

Coordinated hallucination scales with benchmark difficulty, not task type

Across six knowledge benchmarks, C-Hall climbs smoothly with question difficulty — from 2% to ~30% — with no change in task type. MMLU-Pro matches the paper’s GSM8K figure on a plain letter task with no extraction ambiguity.

Consequence for the paper. The prerequisite for safe PCR is not “task-type (knowledge vs math) awareness” but difficulty / reliability-tier awareness. The domain-calibrated threshold mechanism the paper proposes for MMLU domains needs to generalise to a difficulty-calibrated one. This widens the paper’s central safety argument rather than contradicting it.

02Boundary condition

On frontier-hard benchmarks, PCR’s accuracy looks fine while its safety breaks

On MMLU-Pro the frontier is no more accurate on the disagreed tail (~72%) than the cheap consensus is on the agreed set (~73%). PCR still shows a +7–11 pp accuracy gain — but purely from consensus routing, and ~27% of the “trusted” answers are wrong.

PCR’s +7–11 pp gain on MMLU-Pro comes from consensus routing (trusting agreement filters to the easier questions), not from the frontier fixing the hard ones. PCR passes the accuracy check and fails the safety check on the same benchmark at the same time. On the easy benchmarks the opposite limit holds: the cheap models are already near ceiling, so PCR gain is ≈0. PCR’s real value band is the middle (MMLU-like); the paper’s “super-frontier” MMLU result (+13.2 pp) is a best case, not a general one.

03Hypothesis test

Family relatedness: robust on agreement, null on coordinated hallucination — and the binding constraint is capability matching

Same-family cheap pairs agree more often (per-benchmark gaps +11 to +21 pp), but that extra agreement is not disproportionately wrong: the C-Hall gap is +2.1 pp, 95% CI [−2.6, +6.2], in every properly capability-matched comparison.

3a   Agreement rate — within-family pairs agree more

Bootstrap 95% CIs on the (within − cross) gap: ARC +11.3 pp [+7.9, +14.6], OpenBookQA +11.7 [+5.5, +17.8], TruthfulQA +14.7 [+6.0, +23.3], MMLU-Pro +20.7 [+10.0, +31.3], GSM8K +43.9 [+36.3, +51.2] — all exclude zero. MMLU is the exception (−3.0 pp, spans 0). The conservative pair-level pool (3 within vs 4 cross pairs, each one draw) is +10.6 pp, CI [−2.3, +24.7] — real per-benchmark, marginal when each pair counts once.

3b   Coordinated hallucination — no effect in any matched comparison

Within-family cheap models agree substantially more, but that extra agreement is not disproportionately wrong. The intuitive “same training family ⇒ they hallucinate together” hypothesis is not supported by any properly matched comparison. What drives C-Hall is the benchmark (Finding 1), not the family relationship.

3c   Why this is hard to test — capability matching is the binding constraint

Wave 3 was meant to break the “within-family = Groq-hosted” confound with a non-Groq Mistral within-family pair (pair8). It produced a striking-looking result — then undercut it:

At face value pair8’s 38–43% C-Hall vs pair9’s 12–22% is a large within-family effect. But the last column kills it: ministral-3b is simply weak (37% on MMLU-Pro); when it “agrees” with ministral-8b it is often because both defaulted to the same plausible-but-wrong answer — a skill gap, not shared family blind spots, the same failure mode that got pair2/gsm8k excluded. Those four cells are excluded from the family pooling; the closer ARC cells are kept.

This is itself a methods finding. The family hypothesis cannot be tested with off-the-shelf same-series small-model pairs — a 3B + 8B pair is systematically mismatched. It needs two same-family models within ~3 pp solo accuracy on the target benchmark (qwen3.6 / qwen3.8, −0.5 to +2 pp, is the only qualifying pair on the hard benchmarks). Until more exist, the honest answer to “does family relatedness cause coordinated hallucination” is no evidence that it does, from the one clean test available.

04Operational

Measured cost and latency — the saving tracks the escalation rate

PCR cuts cost 60–72% versus always-frontier when escalation is low (≤12%), ~40% at ~28%, and ~10% once a benchmark is hard enough to push escalation past 50%. The paper’s 94.7% figure is MMLU-with-domain-calibration specific.

05Caveat — not re-run

The GSM8K 31.8% C-Hall figure looks answer-budget-sensitive

Every post-instrumentation GSM8K run shows C-Hall 2.6–4.3% and accuracy-when-agreed 95–97%, against the paper’s 31.8% / 24.3%. Likely the 12-token answer budget truncated multi-step solutions before the final number.

With a 12-token cap a worked solution is cut off mid-calculation and extract_number() returns an intermediate value, inflating apparent disagreement and C-Hall. The current pipeline uses 300 tokens. This does not weaken the safety thesis — MMLU-Pro (Finding 1) shows a genuine ~27% C-Hall on a letter task with an adequate budget — but the specific 31.8% number should carry a caveat or be re-established with a controlled 12-vs-300 token A/B before being cited as-is.

Methods delta & limitations

Methods delta

  • TruthfulQA MC1, validation split: the one true option + up to 5 sampled distractors (≤6 total), shuffled per-question so the answer isn’t fixed at A.
  • MMLU-Pro test split: options A–J, dataset category kept as domain. 0–5 of 150–250 cheap responses per cell were empty after retries and count as disagreement.
  • OpenBookQA / CommonsenseQA: standard A–D / A–E letter tasks (CommonsenseQA validation split).
  • Letter extraction generalised to A–J with an ordered fallback.
  • Cheap token budget 256 for the new MC benchmarks and MMLU on the new pairs. Historical pair2/pair3 MMLU used 20/40 and is not re-run.
  • Frontiers: gpt-oss-120b on pair6/pair7; mistral-medium-latest on pair8/pair9 (mistral-large-latest returned 403 tier_not_allowed on the free tier).
  • Capability-mismatch exclusions from family pooling: pair2/gsm8k, pair8/{truthfulqa,mmlu_pro}, pair9/{truthfulqa,mmlu_pro} — shown in the per-cell tables, excluded from the grand family means.

Limitations

  • Pilot N (150–300 per cell); per-benchmark C-Hall CIs are ±3–14 pp.
  • The Groq-hosting confound is only partly addressed. Wave 3 added a non-Groq within-family pair (pair8), but it is capability-mismatched, so the only clean within-family evidence is still pair7 (qwen3.6 / qwen3.8), which is Groq-hosted. A capability-matched, non-Groq within-family pair remains the key missing replicate.
  • One frontier per matched pair-set; Finding 2 is specific to gpt-oss-120b.
  • TruthfulQA reduced to 6 options for comparability; absolute C-Hall would shift with the full MC1 set.
  • pair6/commonsenseqa (16/200) and the last 8 of pair6/openbookqa are unfinished — blocked on the gpt-oss-20b daily token cap.
  • sentence-transformers failed to load, so cosine similarity is null for the new runs — the paper’s own sweep shows binary agreement carries the signal.
